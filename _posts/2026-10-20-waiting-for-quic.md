## Key idea

The paper uses unsolicited QUIC backscatter captured by the UCSD network telescope to study deployment behavior and infrastructure that providers do not directly publish. Observable features such as retransmissions, packet coalescence, and connection IDs reveal differences between operators and changes over time, with findings checked against flow records and active measurements.

## Critique

Using several measurement methods makes the inferences more convincing than relying on a single packet feature or dataset. However, backscatter is generated by attacker-selected targets and spoofed addresses, so the observed servers are not a representative sample of all QUIC traffic or users. The paper is therefore more persuasive as evidence about configurations and infrastructure than as a direct measure of global adoption, and apparent changes should be checked against shifts in which deployments happen to be visible.

## Connections

The [partial-reachability reading](https://gavinnwang.github.io/cs440-blog/2026/10/01/partial-reachability/) similarly shows that observations depend on vantage point and that missing visibility should not automatically be interpreted as absence. Both papers make the measurement process part of the explanation, rather than treating the collected data as an unbiased picture of the Internet.
